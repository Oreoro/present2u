The honest version

AI does not replace jobs. It replaces tasks — and a job is a bundle of tasks.

So the question isn't "will AI take my job?"

It's "which parts of my job can a model draft, and what do I do with the time?"

AI in the Workforce2 / 17

What models are good at

  • Pattern-heavy work at scale
  • First drafts, summaries, translations
  • Turning messy text into structure
  • Code scaffolding and refactors
  • Classification and extraction

The common thread: text in, text out, checked against a pattern.

AI in the Workforce3 / 17

What they are still bad at

  • Judgment under genuine ambiguity
  • Accountability — someone has to own the call
  • Novel context the model has never seen
  • Long-horizon planning
  • Knowing when it is wrong

These are exactly the parts we should keep human.

The measured gains

Controlled studies find 20–40% faster on structured writing and coding tasks.

Gains are largest for less-experienced workers, and near zero on tasks that need judgment.

If a draft takes 2 h and review takes 0.5 h, you save

$$ \frac{2 - 0.5}{2} = 75\% $$

on that task — but task speedups do not automatically become job output.

Roles, skills and risk

AI in the Workforce10 / 17

Roles shift, not vanish

  • Junior work becomes review work
  • Leverage moves to context and verification
  • New roles: evaluation, context engineering, AI operations
  • The scarcest skill is knowing what good looks like
AI in the Workforce11 / 17

The new core skills

  1. Decompose — split work into tasks
  2. Contextualise — give the model what it needs
  3. Verify — check against reality, not vibes
  4. Own — carry the decision and the accountability
AI in the Workforce12 / 17

Risks you must manage

  • Hallucination — confident and wrong
  • Bias — inherited from the data
  • Accountability — who signs off?
  • Data leakage — what leaves your walls
  • Deskilling — losing the craft you rely on
AI in the Workforce13 / 17

A policy for teams

  1. Map your tasks and their risk
  2. Pilot the safe, high-volume ones
  3. Keep a human on every decision
  4. Measure quality, not just speed
  5. Train for review, not just prompting
AI in the Workforce14 / 17

Adoption in phases

  • Assist — individuals use it ad hoc
  • Embed — it sits inside the workflow
  • Redesign — the process is rebuilt around it

Most teams are stuck between Assist and Embed.

What to remember

  • Redesign the process — don't just bolt AI on
